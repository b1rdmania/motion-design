## What the test showed

**Useful, but small:**

- The plain spine, agreed with the user, before any design.
- The evidence ledger. Every claim was sourced, and one quote was checked live.
- Legibility and reading-speed checks. They caught a plan that did not match the render.
- The cold read by a fresh agent. It found three real faults. It was also wrong once, and part of its "tiny text" complaint came from low-resolution sampling.

**Not useful, or harmful:**

- **The treatment, the signature moment and the showreel bar.** These sections push the agent to direct and build the look itself. The agent produced the stock images of the category: a film strip, a red pencil, letters in fog. It put its effort into realistic rendering, not ideas.
- **The device test ran at the wrong stage.** Step 3 tests each telling's device ("would the spine be lost without it?"). No step tests the shots in the treatment the same way. The trailer shots would have failed it.
- **The director built the shots.** No Blender workflow skill exists, so the planning agent scripted every Blender shot by hand. This is the test-1 failure again, in a different tool.
- **Generated music did not follow its prompt**, in either run. We measured the loudness curve and moved each cue into place.

## Options (not decided)

1. **Park it as it is.** This is the current state.
2. **Cut it to a brief-and-review layer:**
   - Agree the story with the user: stakes, then the plain spine.
   - Hand a clean brief to the renderer's own workflow, and do not direct the look.
   - Check the result: claims, readability, cold read.

   Remove the treatment, signature moment, showreel bar and toolchain choice.
3. **Run a baseline before deciding.** Give the same brief to plain Opus with the same tools and no skill. Compare the two films blind. Andy's view is that the result is already clear.
