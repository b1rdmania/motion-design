## What worked

- **The stakes question** ("what is hardest, what does it depend on?"). When it was asked (2b, 3), it surfaced the real tension, and the concept came from it. When it was skipped (2), the film missed it.
- **Checking legibility at viewing width.** In every run it changed a shot: cropped cards, bigger type, killed small print.
- **The evidence ledger.** It caught unsourced lines, stopped sample data being shown as real, and made the dig agent verify every hop against live data.
- **Keeping the renderer's own craft.** The rule "plan owns what and when, tools own how" came from the test-1 failure.
- **The cold-viewer check** (a fresh subagent sees only the frames). It caught late product reveals twice. Its concrete claims are unreliable: it invented details, and gave timestamps 1–2 s off.
- **The review receipts.** Every critique records the render's sha256 and the plan version, and `deliver.py` refuses a critique that doesn't match. A stale or mismatched review cannot satisfy the delivery checks. The hash does not prove that the review itself was useful.
- **The showreel check (`judged.reel_bar`).** Agents failed their own films honestly, and in test 3 the failure triggered a revision round.

## What has not worked

1. **Narrative (the main problem).** Observed: Andy judged every film "a bit weird narratively". The films are built around a device (a clipboard, a trail, a stamp), not around a person's situation.

   Established: no human took part in any story decision. The test clients were models, so every story was proposed and approved by a model.

   Hypotheses (not tested):
   - A model client tends to pick the cleverest option.
   - The tellings prompt ("push at least one option away from the category average") rewards clever devices over plain stories.
   - No plain story spine is required before a device is allowed.

   **The narrative step has never been tested with a real person, so the effect of that absence is not known.**
2. **The showreel bar has never been met.** When it failed, the agent made the smallest fix (in test 3, a camera escalation on the same scene), not a bigger rethink.
3. **Sound can't be verified.** No agent can listen. Agents described sound properties they had not measured. `sound.shape` (a loudness curve) and "measure claims on the final mix" help, but the subjective check always stays open.
4. **Review volume.** PR #3 cut it to one finding per beat. It is still heavy for multi-format delivery: 9:16 needs a whole second review, and carry-over only covers evidence. It is also noisy for continuous-camera films (`fidelity.hold` fires on every beat).
5. **Comprehension trails the baseline.** Plain baseline films said who they were for and what the product is sooner. The 2b result (34–28 for the skill) is encouraging, but comprehension still trailed there, and the baseline's total varied between judges.
6. **Judges vary.** The same baseline film scored 33 and 28 with different judges. One run per arm.
7. **The HyperFrames handoff is heavy.** It involves a lot of reading and clashes with the workflows' own intake and house style (mitigated in the adapter).

## Recommended next steps

1. **Put a person in step 3.** Andy, for one real brief: he picks or rewrites the story himself. This is the test that matters, and it has never been run.
2. **Add a plain story spine to step 3:** situation → problem → turn → what changes, told as something happening to someone. It has to work in plain words before any device is allowed. Rewrite "push away from the category average" so it cannot be satisfied by a clever device alone.
3. **Check comprehension before the showreel bar.** First confirm that a cold viewer knows whose problem this is, what the product does and what changes. Only then judge craft against the showreel bar, so that spectacle is never rewarded before the film makes sense.
4. **When a check fails, revisit the failed decision.** Diagnose whether the failure is in the story, the visual treatment or the execution, and revise that layer. A bigger camera move cannot fix an unclear story. Judge the revision by whether it fixes the diagnosed problem, not by its size.
5. **Reduce review load:** a `"camera": "continuous"` flag in the score, and carry-over across formats for identical plan and audio.
6. **Run the timing fixture through each adapter** and update each adapter's fixture status.

## Test protocol (to reuse)

- **Arms:** same opening request, repo and tools. One arm uses the skill; the baseline does not.
- **Client:** each arm talks to its own client agent through files, with an identical briefing that hides one "stakes" fact, revealed only if asked. The orchestrator passes messages between them.
- **Judging:** a fresh blind judge per comparison scores comprehension, brand fit, phone readability, craft and the showreel bar, and states a preference.
- **Replace the simulated client with Andy for the narrative test.**
