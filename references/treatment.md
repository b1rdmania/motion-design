# Treatment

A treatment records the decisions the renderer needs. Aim for a concise page for a short film; do not repeat the brief or invent fields that add no value. Reuse existing brand and positioning work.

Cover what matters:

- Audience, placement, desired takeaway/action and feeling.
- Proposition: a clear central idea. A conjunction is not evidence of a bad idea.
- Boundaries: what must not be shown, implied or exposed.
- Ending: the last image, line or sound. Writing it early can clarify purpose; another starting point is valid.
- Concept: explore alternatives if the creative question is unresolved. A supplied concept need not be ceremonially rejected and rediscovered.
- References: specific real works, with what to borrow and avoid, when useful. No required count. Do not claim to have inspected a reference you have not seen.
- Brand: authoritative source, palette, typography, logo and mandatory details. References do not authorize an unrelated identity.
- Motion language and lead: optional verbs or principles; music-, narration- or visual-led timing.
- Delivery: formats, fps, duration, viewing size and any actual sound/accessibility requirements.

Record assumed decisions briefly. The evidence ledger sits beside the treatment. Optional review-hint overrides belong in the score; do not duplicate them in two documents.

For a material change to the agreed plan, add `## Amendments` with the date, decision and reason. Then re-run the affected review. Do not amend a requirement solely to erase a genuine failure. Routine implementation choices do not require a formal amendment or another approval.
